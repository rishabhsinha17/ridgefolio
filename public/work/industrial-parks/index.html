Mapping Indonesia’s industrial parks

The research team had a spreadsheet full of evidence about Indonesia’s industrial parks. Rishabh Sinha built the website that lets readers explore it, then gave the team a way to publish the next edition.

From spreadsheet to public map

An operating park might draw power from the national grid, from a captive coal plant or from both. Readers needed to switch from park status to electricity supply without losing their place on the map. When researchers corrected a record, the downloadable file needed to reflect the same change.

A map and a set of research charts

Rishabh connected the ArcGIS web map to thematic GeoJSON files. A reader can select a park to inspect its electricity supply or look up the sources behind a reported dispute. He also implemented nine Plotly charts from CSV data, including regional status and electricity capacity by industry. April 2025 chart code

A repeatable update

Rishabh wrote a Python script that groups the master CSV fields into seven geographic files. He updated the public CSV download to use the master file and connected the site to the team’s selected ArcGIS map. A maintainer could regenerate the files, update the hosted layers and check the published map against the download. Generation script · Download and map changes

Corrections after launch

The team added Indonesia Huabao Industrial Park and updated the public methodology in October 2024. Rishabh added a contribution page with English and Bahasa Indonesia forms. His handover work with Molly Schreier covered replacing a map layer while preserving its colors and symbols. CGS methodology · Contribution page

June 2024 Public launch
CGS presented a downloadable dataset of 140 parks with six map layers. The launch deck shows the interface and how to read each layer. Launch presentation
May 2025 Engineering handover
The archived master table contains 141 named parks. Each of the seven geographic files contains the same 141 parks, with fields selected for its topic. Inspect the files
December 2025 Research reuse
CGS’s subsequent study of 106 nickel smelters lists the industrial parks dataset among the sources used to identify a smelter’s industrial park. This documents reuse of the team’s data. Methods, Appendix II

Project records

Rishabh built the website as a University of Maryland research assistant, working with Molly Schreier and Jiehong Lou. CGS and its collaborators collected and validated the data. The archived site credits the research team and the website creator separately.
